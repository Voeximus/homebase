Homebase — the whole app

Every screen, one design. Scroll, or jump.

Open & sign in Finance Sheets Health
Shell — what you see before anything else
Open

Every cold launch. The two doors are the whole app; the strip underneath is where you stand, so the screen is worth the tap it costs you. The numbers come from the same place Home reads them, so the two can never disagree.

Sign in

Once per device. Nothing on it but the mark, the name and the form.

Finance — four tabs
Home

One hero, at a size nothing else gets. The old Home gave seven facts the same weight, so it had no opinion about what mattered. This one answers the question you actually open the app with — what can I spend — then the next three dated things, then the envelopes, then what just happened. Debt is still here; it stopped being the headline because it moves once a fortnight and your spending moves every day.

Insights

Where it went, and whether that is normal. The donut names its five biggest slices and nothing more; the comparison row underneath is the part that is actually useful — this cycle against your own average, not against a budget you set once.

Activity

The ledger. Needs-review is a filter, not a nag — and a charge only lands there when the app genuinely cannot tell, never when it already picked a category. Pending charges are marked inline rather than shoved into the review pile.

Profile

Accounts, connections, and the switches. Grouped so the destructive things are not next to the harmless ones.

Sheets — what opens on top
A transaction

Tap any row. The category is a choice you can change in one tap, not a form. If the app guessed, it says so and shows you why.

Bills

What is due, in date order, with the ones already paid greyed rather than hidden — so you can see the month, not just the remainder.

Health — four tabs
Today

Same rule as Home: one hero. Calories left, the ring, then the three macros as a fixed legend that never changes colour between screens.

Add food

Search, scan, or reuse. The scanner is the primary action because it is the one that actually gets used.

Workouts

The session as a list of sets you tick off, with last time's numbers beside each one so you know what to beat.

Progress

Weight trend, the week, and whether the plan is working — one screen, no configuration.

This is one design, not a menu. Tell me what is wrong with it and I will change that thing — "the hero should be debt not spend", "too dark", "I hate the donut", "rows are too tall". It is a flat page, so none of it is in the app yet and changes are cheap.
